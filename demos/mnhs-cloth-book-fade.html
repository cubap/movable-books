Fergus Falls Woolen Mill cloth sample book — flap states

A cloth sample book (Minnesota Historical Society, 1896) with fabric swatches mounted on hinged flaps. Printed details sit under each flap. Drag the slider or press Play to move through the four photographed states with a gentle crossfade.

About this object & the states
  • 01 — Closed cover: “No. 3. CLOTH SAMPLES” from the Fergus Falls Woolen Mill (1896).
  • 02 — Open spread; the twelve cloth samples are mounted on hinged flaps, all down.
  • 03 — A flap on the right page is lifted, revealing the printed style text underneath.
  • 04 — The top-left flap on the left page is lifted above the page edge, revealing “STYLE 37 — Suiting” print. The backside of the cloth (lighter, less finished) is visible.
Why a fade instead of a physical fold

The underside of the cloth was not photographed flat: glue and fabric thickness cause bowing and shadowing, and each hinged sample has different tension. These photographs are well-registered at the same size, so a crossfade is a pragmatic, artifact-friendly way to move between states without modeling hinge physics (see photography recommendations below).

Photography recommendations
  • Keep camera position, exposure, and white balance locked across all states so crossfades align without flicker (this set is already well-registered — keep doing that).
  • Include a gray card or color target in at least one frame per setup for color correction.
  • For the cloth backside: even if it can’t lie flat, capture it with raking light to document texture; a separate flat photo of a detached sample would let it be composited.
  • Capture each intermediate state (each flap individually, then combinations) rather than only end states.
